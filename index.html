League fingerprints

The radar is for comparing overall profiles; areas and angles are not a reliable read of magnitude. For precise values use the line and bar charts. Every axis is scaled independently across the five leagues, so a shape shows relative position, not absolute size. “Title gap” is the average winning margin — a larger value means a less competitive league.

Goals & style: converging or diverging?

Research question 2. Are the Big Five drifting toward one globalised style, or holding their own identities? We follow scoring, shot volume and finishing efficiency.

Volume vs reward

Each point is one league-season (2005/06–2025/26): shots per game (x) against goals per game (y). Up-and-left = clinical; down-and-right = wasteful. Bubble size = shots on target.

Finishing efficiency

Goals scored per 100 shots. Higher = more clinical leagues.

Whistles, fouls & cards

Research question 3. Southern Europe has a reputation for theatre and yellow cards; the north for letting play flow. Do the numbers agree? We separate how physical a league is (fouls) from how strictly it is refereed (cards per foul).

The temperament map

One point per league-season (2005/06–2025/26): fouls per game on the x-axis, cards per game on the y-axis. Above the dashed line = more cards than the same number of fouls usually earns, i.e. stricter refereeing; below it = more lenient. The dashed line is a reference ratio — the Big-Five average cards-per-foul rate through the origin — not a fitted regression.

Referee leniency

Fouls committed per yellow card shown, 2005/06–2025/26. Higher = more fouls tolerated before a booking = more lenient; lower = stricter.

Title races: contest or coronation?

Research question 4. Some leagues are genuine sprints to the line; others are annual coronations. We measure the winning margin and let you drill into any final table.

Winning margin over the runner-up

Points separating the champion from the runner-up, by season. Large margins = one-team seasons; small margins = highly competitive races. A margin of zero means the two finished level on points and the title went to the league's tie-break, not that it was necessarily decided on the final day.

Who actually won

Official league titles, 2000/01–2025/26. Standings are recomputed from results with each league's own tie-break rule, then corrected where a title was decided off the field. A handful of clubs dominate an entire continent.

Data & tools. Source: github.com/datasets/football-datasets (match-level results & stats for the Premier League, La Liga, Serie A, Bundesliga and Ligue 1). Pre-processing in Python (pandas + NumPy): 130 season files aggregated into 130 league-season rows, standings recomputed from results, every metric normalised per match, and rates that are divided by one another computed over the same set of matches. Visualisation built from scratch with D3.js v7 (SVG), vanilla JS and CSS. Colours are the Okabe–Ito colour-blind-safe set, used unmodified. Simulated worst-case pair separation is ΔE 23.8 under protanopia, 16.4 under deuteranopia and 10.7 under tritanopia, so colour alone is not relied on: every series carries a text label and every mark a tooltip.
